On the crucifixion
-------------------
Jesus was crucified under Pontius Pilate. The Roman historian Tacitus records
that "Christus, the founder of the name, was put to death by Pontius Pilatus,
procurator of Judea, in the reign of Tiberius." His death by crucifixion is one
of the most certain facts of ancient history.

On the burial
--------------
And when evening had come, Joseph of Arimathea, a respected member of the
Sanhedrin, took courage and went to Pilate and asked for the body of Jesus. He
bought a linen shroud, and taking him down, wrapped him in the linen shroud and
laid him in a tomb that had been cut out of the rock.

On the empty tomb
-----------------
When the Sabbath was past, Mary Magdalene and the other women came to the tomb
early on the first day of the week. They found the stone rolled away, and the
tomb was empty. A messenger said to them, "He is not here, for he has risen."
That the empty tomb was first discovered by women is striking, since in that
culture the testimony of women carried little legal weight.

On the appearances
------------------
For I delivered to you as of first importance what I also received: that Christ
died for our sins, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day, and
that he appeared to Cephas, then to the twelve. Then he appeared to more than
five hundred at one time. Last of all he appeared also to Paul. This creed in
1 Corinthians 15 is datable to within a few years of the events.

A naturalistic reading
----------------------
Some scholars argue these appearances are best explained as grief-induced
visions. Bereavement hallucinations are well documented, and cognitive
dissonance can transform a movement after the loss of its leader. On this view
the resurrection accounts show legendary development in the later Gospels rather
than a literal bodily resurrection.
